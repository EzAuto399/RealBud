# RealBud — PM discovery and first-use worksheet

Prepared 6 September 2026. Allow roughly 40–50 minutes. Use the questions as prompts; skip anything already answered. Let the PM explain their work before showing a relevant example.

**Meeting outcome:** choose one worthwhile recurring workflow, understand how it actually works, and agree what is needed to test it with one named PM. Additional workflows go on a later list.

4. **“What does your current software already handle well, and where do you still have to do the work yourself?”**
5. **“If we could take one recurring job off your plate first, which would make the biggest difference?”**

## 2. Walk through one actual job

6. **“Could you show me the last time you did that, with private details hidden?”**
   Follow the trigger, screens or files, decisions, output, and final filing step. Observe before suggesting improvements.
7. **“How often does this happen, and roughly how much hands-on time does each one take?”**
   Separate active work from waiting on someone. Ask about busy periods and the consequence of a delay or mistake.
8. **“How do you know the information is current and belongs to the right property or tenancy?”**
   If sources disagree, ask which record they trust and what they check next.
9. **“What makes a case straightforward, and what makes you stop and handle it differently?”**
   Ask for one recent exception; identify urgency, missing information, owner-specific rules and professional judgment.
10. **“What has to happen before you consider this job finished?”**
    Ask where the result belongs, who needs to see it, and how they know a colleague has not already handled it.

## 3. Tune the help to their working style

11. **“Could you show me a good example of the finished result—a message, comparison, checklist, or report?”**
    With permission, request a redacted example and relevant template. Ask about tone, language, required facts and owner preferences.
12. **“Which parts would you be comfortable having Bud check or prepare, and where would you want it to stop for you?”**
    Record preferences without promising unsupported actions. Keep the first trial to checking and preparation with PM review.
13. **“What would you need to see to review its work quickly and trust it?”**
    Prompts: source links, source dates, calculations, missing facts, changes since last time.
14. **“When should this be ready, and what deserves an interruption rather than waiting for your next review?”**
    Include office hours, after-hours handling, escalation recipient and availability at the computer for portal work.
15. **“If information is missing, a login expires, or only half the job is done, how should it hand the work back to you?”**
    Ask who takes over when the PM is unavailable and what context they need to continue.

## 4. Make the first trial practical

16. **“Which systems and computer would we need for this job, and who can approve access or provide an export?”**
    Record actual product/account names and the Mac/Windows environment. Complete the office checklist below only where relevant.
17. **“What could we use for a small first trial, and are there office rules about using that information with AI?”**
    Agree a minimal sample and who can approve its use. Clarify storage, retention, deletion and any provider restrictions before sharing real records.
18. **“What would make the trial worth continuing—and what would make you stop using it?”**
    Agree a time-saving target, acceptable review effort, quality criteria and unacceptable mistakes. Ask what could make adoption feel like extra work.
19. **“Who would use it first, and who else needs to agree to setup and any ongoing cost?”**
    Establish the decision maker and purchasing process. If appropriate: “If it proves useful, what budget or approval process would we work within?”
20. **“Can we choose the first job, who will provide the sample, and a date to try it together?”**
    Agree a first-use session, support contact and review date. Ask the PM to complete one run themselves during onboarding.

## Optional prompts — use only for their chosen job

| Workflow | Follow-up questions |
| --- | --- |
| Owner updates | Which owners need updates, when, and in what language or tone? Which facts must be included? Which situations need a call instead? |
| Maintenance and quote comparison | How do you spot emergencies? What scope, exclusions, GST and access details must a comparison include? Who approves spending, and who follows up? |
| Rent/payment checking | Which ledger or export is authoritative, and how fresh is it? How do you handle partial payments, unallocated receipts or agreed payment plans? Who confirms the next action? |
| Daily priorities and follow-ups | Where do commitments arrive? How do you track items waiting on an owner, tenant or contractor? What should carry over to tomorrow? |
| Renewals and inspections | Where are dates recorded and checked? Who verifies applicable requirements? What preparation would help before the authorised person handles notices or decisions? |
| Portal work | Which exact site and account? Which pages are read and which steps change something? Who signs in and handles MFA? Can we arrange an approved test environment? Can the PM be at the screen? |

These are candidate workflows, not claims that their live systems are already connected or verified. Ask, Desk and Schedule can be introduced around the chosen outcome after discovery.

## After the meeting — turn answers into configuration and evidence

1. Translate the chosen job into its sources, steps, rules, exclusions, output, schedule and human handoff. Separate office defaults from property-specific exceptions. Confirm assumptions with the PM before approving the job.
2. Start with an approved redacted or minimal sample. Check the installed environment and actual access path before making a live-system promise. Choose an export/file path when suitable; portal work needs its own demonstrated access and attended run.
3. Compare three representative cases: a normal case, an exception, and a missing/conflicting-information case. This is an initial usefulness check, not a reliability benchmark.
4. Let the PM provide the input, review/correct the result and continue the work themselves. Observe where they hesitate, repeat explanations or need assistance. Show stop, retry, pending-review and partial-result handling.
5. Measure **net PM time returned = baseline active time − input, supervision, review, correction and handoff time**. Record setup time separately, material errors, missing facts and whether the result was usable.
6. At the agreed review, decide together: continue, adjust, or stop. Keep unproven integrations and additional workflows on a separate follow-up list.

“Tuning” here means workflow instructions, templates, property rules, timing and access scope. This worksheet does not authorise model training on agency data.
